Smorelore

A state of mind. Part campfire, part workbench, part story told after dark.

Around the fire

Camping strips things down. A fire, a few friends, a stick, a marshmallow. Nobody's in a hurry. You watch the coals, you listen, and somebody starts telling a story that's been told before and is better every time.

That's the feeling this place is built around: being one with nature for a little while, and noticing how much joy fits inside simple things.

A s'more is three ingredients and a little patience. Most good things are.

Back to the workbench

Then you come home and open a laptop, and everything is loud again. Frameworks on frameworks. Features nobody asked for. Software that wants your attention more than it wants to help you.

Smorelore is about carrying the campfire back to the craft:

  • Small. Make the thing that does one job well.
  • Patient. Toast it slowly. Don't torch it.
  • Shared. Made to be passed around the circle.
  • Honest. No dark patterns, no tricks, nothing hidden in the woods.

The old engineer

Every campfire has one: the person who has seen a few things. Systems that rose and fell. Ideas that were "new" three times. Bugs that turned into legends.

That wisdom tends to get lost, because it lives in stories, not in docs. Lore is where it goes. This site is a place to write it down before the fire burns out.

Looking forward from the fire

The future of tech is moving fast, and a lot of it is genuinely amazing. The question around this fire isn't whether to use the new tools. It's how to use them and stay human: build with care, keep things simple, and leave the campsite better than you found it.

What this could become

Unsure yet, and that's fine. Some kindling:

  • StoriesShort tales from a career in engineering. The bug, the launch, the lesson.
  • Small appsLike Math Facts. Little tools, made carefully, that each do one thing.
  • Field notesObservations from the trail and the terminal, side by side.
  • An art projectSomething where code is the medium and the campfire is the subject. A generative fire that grows as stories are added?
  • An idea logThe endless ideas, written down as sparks. Most will float off. A few will catch.

Pull up a log

For now it's just this page and a fire. More to come.
